Chess

Chess is one of the world’s oldest and most strategic board games, celebrated for its blend of intellect, creativity, and competition. Originating in northern India around the 6th century as chaturanga, it spread through Persia and the Islamic world before reaching Europe, where it evolved into the modern form we know today. Played on an 8×8 checkered board with 64 squares, chess involves two players controlling 16 pieces each — pawns, rooks, knights, bishops, a queen, and a king — with the ultimate goal of checkmating the opponent’s king.Chess is a timeless game of strategy and intellect, played and admired across cultures for centuries.

Basic Rules of Chess
  1. Objective: Place the opponent’s king under attack (checkmate) so they have no legal move.
  2. Board setup: 64 squares, alternating light and dark. Each player has 16 pieces: 1 king, 1 queen, 2 rooks, 2 bishops, 2 knights, and 8 pawns.
  3. First move: White always moves first, then players alternate.
  4. Winning: Win by checkmate, opponent resignation, or time-forfeit.

Piece Movements
  1. King: One square in any direction.
  2. Queen: Any number of squares in any direction.
  3. Rook: Any number of squares horizontally or vertically.
  4. Bishop: Any number of squares diagonally.
  5. Knight: Moves in an “L” shape (two squares in one direction, then one perpendicular). Can jump over pieces.
  6. Pawn: Moves forward one square (two on its first move), captures diagonally, and can promote upon reaching the opposite end. Special moves include en passant and promotion.
Competitive Rules
  1. Chess clock: Each player has a set time; exceeding it loses the game.
  2. Notation: Moves must be recorded in algebraic notation during official games.
  3. Conduct: Players must not distract or disturb opponents.
  4. Variants: Rapid, Blitz, and Chess960 have special timing and setup rules.

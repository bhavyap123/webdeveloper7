  • Life's Amazing Secrets

    How to Find Balance and Purpose in Your Life by Gaur Gopal Das uses the metaphor of the four wheels of a car to outline the four interdependent domains of a balanced existence: personal life, relationships, work life, and social contribution. Framed around a conversation during a Mumbai traffic jam, the book presents ancient spiritual insights through contemporary anecdotes.


  • The Psychology of Money

    Morgan Housel's The Psychology of Money posits that financial success is driven more by human behavior and soft skills than by technical intelligence or spreadsheet optimization. The book emphasizes that true wealth is found in unseen savings and the personal freedom to control one's time rather than in chasing maximum market returns. You can read more about the book's core insights on Amazon.


  • Wings of Fire: An Autobiography

    "Wings of Fire" is the deeply inspiring 1999 autobiography of Dr. A.P.J. Abdul Kalam, the eminent aerospace scientist and 11th President of India. Co-authored with Arun Tiwari, the book chronicles Kalam’s journey from a humble, middle-class childhood in Rameswaram, Tamil Nadu, to his instrumental role in leading India’s space research and missile development programmes, earning him the title "Missile Man of India".


  • The Palace of Illusions

    The Palace of Illusions by Chitra Banerjee Divakaruni is a feminist reimagining of the ancient Indian epic, the Mahabharata, narrated entirely from the perspective of Panchaali (Draupadi). The novel reframes a patriarchal myth into a deeply personal story of a woman navigating power, choice, and destiny in a world ruled by men and gods.


  • The Blue Umbrella

    The Blue Umbrella is a heartwarming 1974 children's novella written by renowned Indian author Ruskin Bond. Set in a picturesque, small mountain village in the Garhwal hills of Uttarakhand, the story explores deep human emotions like materialistic desire, envy, kindness, and the power of forgiveness through the eyes of a simple village girl.

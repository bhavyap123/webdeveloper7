Durga Puja - The Festival of Strength and Devotion

What is Durga Puja?

Durga Puja is a major Hindu festival celebrated primarily in West Bengal, Odisha, Assam, and Bangladesh. It honors Goddess Durga’s victory over the demon Mahishasura, symbolizing the triumph of good over evil.

Why is it Celebrated?

The festival marks the return of Goddess Durga to her maternal home and commemorates her powerful battle against Mahishasura. It reflects devotion, strength, and the divine feminine energy.

How is Durga Puja Celebrated?

  • Elaborate pandals (temporary temples) are set up
  • Idols of Goddess Durga are worshipped for five days
  • Cultural performances, music, and dance fill the streets
  • Special rituals like Pushpanjali, Sandhi Puja, and Sindoor Khela are performed
  • On the last day, idols are immersed in rivers (Visarjan)

Key Days of Celebration

  • Shashthi: Welcoming the goddess
  • Saptami: Beginning of rituals
  • Ashtami: Kumari Puja and Sandhi Puja
  • Navami: Final day of worship
  • Dashami: Farewell and immersion of idols

Fun Fact

Durga Puja in Kolkata was added to UNESCO’s Intangible Cultural Heritage list in 2021.
